Iruvathettu

The 28 card game Malayali families play at home, now at a private table on your phone. Open a table, send one invite, and sit down with friends and family in any city. There is no account to make.

How it works

  • Send one invite

    Open a table and share the link or the six-character code. Each person types a name and takes a seat. No email, phone number, password or sign-in is needed.

  • Bots for empty seats

    Two or three of you tonight? A bot takes each empty seat, so the evening still happens. Bots are labelled as bots everywhere they appear, and you can also practise against them.

  • Kerala 28 rules

    The rules follow Pagat's description of Kerala 28: a 32-card deck, bidding, trumps, points for the jack, nine, ace and ten, and 28 points in all. The game shows which rules it uses, so nobody has to argue about how it goes.

  • House rules everyone can see

    Before the first deal, the host switches on or off the few rules your family plays differently: the pair of king and queen of trumps, second-round bidding after all eight cards, and a match target of 6, 10 or 15 game points. Everyone sees the settings before the game starts.

  • Cards that feel real

    Cards are dealt with animation, played to the centre of the table and collected by the winner of each trick. Short, clear sounds mark each move, so you can follow every hand. Your cards stay hidden from everyone else's phone.

  • Reconnect and carry on

    If your connection drops, a bot plays your turns until you return. Reopen the app and you are back in your own seat, with your hand, and the game carries on.

  • Scores at a glance

    After each hand, see the bid, who made it, the points each side took and the running match score.

  • English or Malayalam

    Choose English or Malayalam in Settings, or follow your phone's language.

  • Talk on your own call

    Iruvathettu has no voice chat and never asks for your microphone. Start a WhatsApp or phone call with your group and keep the table open in front of you.
